GRAMMAR · 7 MIN READ

English grammar
for beginners.

Grammar is the structure that helps words make sense together. Begin with a few building blocks and grow from there.

✳By the Lingolift teamFor English beginners
THE BUILDING BLOCKSS+V→A sentence takes shape.

English grammar can seem like a long list of rules. A more comfortable way to begin is to look at the basic structure of a sentence and understand what each part contributes.

What does grammar do?

Grammar describes how words change and fit together. It gives a sentence an order that helps a reader or listener understand the meaning. You don't need to memorise every rule before you can begin using English.

Start with a subject and a verb

A subject tells us who or what a sentence is about. A verb tells us what the subject does or is.

A SIMPLE SENTENCE

Birds sing.

Subject + verb

In this example, birds is the subject and sing is the verb. Add more information to make the idea fuller: “Birds sing in the morning.” The core sentence still has its subject and verb.

Notice basic word order

Many simple English sentences follow this order: subject, verb, then the rest of the idea. For example, “Maya reads a book.” Maya is the subject, reads is the verb, and a book completes the thought.

As you read or listen, notice this order in sentences around you. Finding familiar patterns can help grammar feel less abstract.

Use verb tense to show time

Verb forms can show when something happens. “I walk” can describe a regular activity. “I walked” describes something in the past. Begin by noticing the verb and the time being described; practise one pattern at a time.

Learn through examples

When a grammar idea is new, look at a few clear examples and try making a sentence of your own. Keep it simple, then add detail as your confidence grows. Revisit ideas that take time to settle.

For more beginner-friendly English grammar learning and structured practice, you can learn about grammar development in the Lingolift app.
