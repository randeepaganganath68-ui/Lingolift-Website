VOCABULARY · 5 MIN READ

Essential English
vocabulary.

A bigger vocabulary grows one useful word at a time. Try these simple ways to make new English words easier to remember and use.

✳By the Lingolift teamFor English beginners
WORDS THAT STICKword wordwordCollect words. Make them yours.

Learning vocabulary isn't about memorising the biggest list. It's about finding useful words, meeting them in context, and practising until they feel familiar.

Choose words you'll actually use

Start with words related to your daily life: people, places, routines, interests, and the things you want to say. Useful words give you more chances to practise and help you build sentences that feel relevant.

Try keeping a small list of words you encounter while reading or listening. Add a short meaning or example sentence so each word has context.

Learn words in context

A word's surrounding sentence can reveal how it's used. Rather than learning an isolated translation alone, notice the phrase or situation where the word appears.

A WORD IN A SENTENCE

“I'm curious about how plants grow.”

curious: wanting to know more

Now make your own example. Personal sentences connect a new word to something meaningful and make it easier to recall later.

Return to words over time

Seeing a word once is rarely enough. Revisit your list after a short break, cover the meaning and see what you remember, or try using a word in a new sentence. Short review sessions can fit into an ordinary day.

Learn related words together

Some words naturally belong together. You could learn the words for a routine, a place, or an activity as a small group. Keep each group manageable so the new vocabulary remains easy to revisit.

Practise using what you know

Write a few simple sentences, describe an object around you, or say a short thought aloud. It is fine to use a small vocabulary while you're learning. Each new word gives you another way to express yourself.
